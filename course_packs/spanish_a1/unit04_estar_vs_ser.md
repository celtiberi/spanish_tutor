# Unit 4 — *Estar*, and *Ser* vs *Estar*

## Learning objectives

1. Conjugate *estar* in the present tense.
2. Use *estar* for location and for feelings/temporary states.
3. Choose correctly between *ser* and *estar* in the A1 core cases.
4. Recognize the meaning-changing pairs *ser aburrido/estar aburrido*, *ser listo/estar listo* (recognition only).

## Canonical explanation

### *Estar* — present tense

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| yo | **estoy** |
| tú | **estás** |
| él / ella / usted | **está** |
| nosotros/as | **estamos** |
| vosotros/as | **estáis** |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | **están** |

Note the written accents (*estás, está, estáis, están*) — they mark stress and are part of correct spelling.

### What *estar* is for (at A1)

- **Location** of people and things: *Estoy en casa. El banco está en la plaza. ¿Dónde estás?*
- **Feelings and temporary states:** *Estoy cansado/cansada. Está enferma. Estamos bien.*

### The A1 decision rule

Ask: **what kind of information am I giving?**

| Information | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Who/what someone *is* — identity, origin, profession | **ser** | *Es profesora. Soy de Cuba.* |
| What something is *like* — inherent description | **ser** | *La casa es grande.* |
| **Where** someone/something is | **estar** | *La casa está en Lima.* |
| **How** someone feels / a state right now | **estar** | *Estoy nervioso.* |

A useful A1 slogan: **"ser = what it is; estar = how it is / where it is."** The common "permanent vs temporary" shortcut breaks down quickly (see M-4.2) — prefer the what/how/where framing.

**One systematic exception to teach explicitly:** the location of an **event** uses *ser* (*La fiesta es en mi casa* — "the party is [takes place] at my house"). At A1, learners only need to recognize this; physical objects and people always use *estar* for location.

### Meaning-changing pairs (recognition only)

- *Es aburrido* = he/it is boring. *Está aburrido* = he is bored.
- *Es listo* = he is clever. *Está listo* = he is ready.

## Examples and non-examples

- ✅ *Mi madre es doctora y ahora está en el hospital.* — profession with *ser*, location with *estar*.
- ✅ *¿Cómo estás? — Estoy muy bien.*
- ❌ *La escuela es en la calle Juárez.* — physical location: *está* (see M-4.1).
- ❌ *Soy cansado.* — feeling/state: *Estoy cansado* (see M-4.3).
- ❌ *Estoy estudiante.* — identity: *Soy estudiante.*

## Common misconceptions

### M-4.1 — Location uses *ser* because it's permanent
\**Madrid es en España* — "Madrid is permanently in Spain, so *ser*." **Diagnosis cue:** *ser* used for where something is, often with a "permanence" justification. **Remediation:** location of things/people is *always* estar, permanent or not: *Madrid está en España*. This is the strongest evidence against the permanent/temporary shortcut — use it to install the what/where rule.

### M-4.2 — "Ser = permanent, estar = temporary" always works
The shortcut misfires both directions (\**es muerto* is wrong even though death is permanent — *está muerto*; recognition-level aside). **Diagnosis cue:** learner justifies a wrong choice by permanence. **Remediation:** replace the shortcut with the what / how / where question from the decision table; re-test with *Madrid está en España* and *Estoy cansado*.

### M-4.3 — English "I am X" always maps to *ser*
\**Soy cansado, soy enfermo, soy bien.* **Diagnosis cue:** *ser* with a feeling/state adjective, or \**soy bien*. **Remediation:** feelings and current states take *estar*; *bien/mal* only ever go with *estar* at this level. Drill: *Estoy cansado / enferma / bien / mal.*

### M-4.4 — Accents on *está/estás* are optional
Learner writes *esta* for *está*. **Diagnosis cue:** missing accents on estar forms in writing. **Remediation:** *esta* (this, fem.) and *está* (is) are different words; the accent is meaningful. Quick contrast: *Esta casa está en la playa.*

### M-4.5 — *ser* and *estar* are interchangeable because both mean "to be"
Learner picks whichever comes to mind, guessing 50/50. **Diagnosis cue:** inconsistent choices with no stated reason. **Remediation:** don't correct item-by-item; teach the decision table, then have the learner *explain the reason* (what / how / where) before answering each drill item.

## Practice items

**P-4.1** — Conjugate *estar* for: yo, tú, ella, nosotros, ustedes.
**Key:** *estoy, estás, está, estamos, están.*

**P-4.2** — *Ser* or *estar*? Fill and justify in one word (what/how/where): *(1) Mi hermano ___ ingeniero. (2) Los niños ___ en el parque. (3) ___ (yo) muy cansada hoy. (4) La ciudad ___ bonita.*
**Key:** *(1) es — what. (2) están — where. (3) Estoy — how. (4) es — what (inherent description).*

**P-4.3** — Correct the errors: \**Mi amiga es en casa porque es enferma.*
**Key:** *Mi amiga está en casa porque está enferma.*

**P-4.4** — Translate: "The party is at Ana's house."
**Key:** *La fiesta es en la casa de Ana.* (Event location → *ser*; accept *en casa de Ana*.)

**P-4.5 (production)** — Write four sentences about a family member: who they are, what they're like, where they are now, how they feel today.
**Key (model):** *Mi hermana es doctora. Es muy inteligente. Está en el trabajo. Está cansada hoy.* Check the verb choice matches each information type.

## Scope notes

- Do not introduce the present progressive (*estar + -ando/-iendo*) — out of scope.
- The meaning-changing pairs are recognition-only; do not drill production of them.
