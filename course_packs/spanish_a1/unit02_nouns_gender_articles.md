# Unit 2 — Nouns, Gender, Articles, Plurals

## Learning objectives

1. Classify common nouns as masculine or feminine using ending patterns.
2. Choose the correct definite article (*el, la, los, las*) and indefinite article (*un, una, unos, unas*).
3. Form plurals of regular nouns.
4. Recognize the highest-frequency exceptions (*el día, la mano, el mapa, el problema, la foto, la moto*).

## Canonical explanation

### Gender

Every Spanish noun is grammatically masculine or feminine — including things (*el libro* the book, *la mesa* the table). Gender is a property of the word, not the object.

**Default patterns:**

- Ends in **-o** → usually masculine: *el libro, el vaso, el amigo*
- Ends in **-a** → usually feminine: *la casa, la silla, la amiga*
- Ends in **-ción / -sión / -dad / -tad** → feminine: *la nación, la ciudad, la libertad*
- Ends in **-e** or a consonant → unpredictable; learn the article with the word: *el carro* (also *el auto*; Spain: *el coche*), *la noche, el papel, la flor*

**High-frequency exceptions (memorize):**

| Word | Meaning | Note |
|------|---------|------|
| **el día** | day | -a but masculine |
| **el mapa** | map | -a but masculine |
| **el problema** | problem | Greek-origin -ma words are masculine (*el idioma, el programa, el sistema*) |
| **la mano** | hand | -o but feminine |
| **la foto** | photo | short for *la fotografía* |
| **la moto** | motorcycle | short for *la motocicleta* |

People-nouns usually pair: *el amigo / la amiga*, *el profesor / la profesora*, *el estudiante / la estudiante* (article does the work when the ending doesn't change).

### Articles

| | Definite ("the") | Indefinite ("a/an", pl. "some") |
|---|---|---|
| masc. sing. | **el** | **un** |
| fem. sing. | **la** | **una** |
| masc. pl. | **los** | **unos** |
| fem. pl. | **las** | **unas** |

The article must agree with the noun in **gender and number**: *el libro → los libros*; *una casa → unas casas*.

Do not confuse **el** (the) with **él** (he) — the accent distinguishes them.

### Plurals

- Ends in a vowel → add **-s**: *libro → libros*, *casa → casas*
- Ends in a consonant → add **-es**: *papel → papeles*, *ciudad → ciudades*
- Ends in **-z** → change to **-ces**: *lápiz → lápices*
- Nouns ending in stressed **-ión** lose the written accent in the plural: *nación → naciones*, *lección → lecciones* (the stress stays on the same syllable; with -es added, the accent mark is no longer needed by the spelling rules — at A1, just memorize the pattern).

## Examples and non-examples

- ✅ *la ciudad → las ciudades*
- ✅ *el problema difícil* — masculine despite -a.
- ❌ *la problema* — see M-2.2.
- ❌ *los lápizs* — plural of -z is *lápices*.
- ❌ *un casa* — agreement failure; *una casa*.

## Common misconceptions

### M-2.1 — "-o masculine / -a feminine" is an absolute rule
Learner applies the ending rule to exceptions: *la mano* becomes \**el mano*, *el día* becomes \**la día*. **Diagnosis cue:** wrong article on a listed exception word. **Remediation:** confirm the default rule is good, then drill the six exception words in this unit as a closed set.

### M-2.2 — Greek -ma words are feminine
\**la problema*, \**la programa*. **Diagnosis cue:** feminine article on *problema, idioma, programa, sistema*. **Remediation:** present the -ma family as a group; have the learner produce *el problema / los problemas* and one original sentence.

### M-2.3 — Gender comes from the object, not the word
Learner reasons "a dress is feminine so *vestido* must be *la*." **Diagnosis cue:** learner justifies an article by the thing's real-world associations. **Remediation:** gender is a property of the *word*; *el vestido* (dress) is masculine, *la corbata* (necktie) is feminine. Reinforce: learn noun + article together.

### M-2.4 — Plural just adds -s always
\**papels*, \**ciudads*. **Diagnosis cue:** missing -es on consonant-final nouns. **Remediation:** vowel → -s, consonant → -es, -z → -ces; drill with *papel, ciudad, lápiz*.

### M-2.5 — él and el are the same word
Learner writes *él libro* or reads *el* as "he." **Diagnosis cue:** accent misuse on el/él in writing. **Remediation:** *el* = "the" (no accent), *él* = "he" (accent). Minimal pair: *el libro* vs *él*; full sentence using only known patterns once *ser* is available: *Él es el amigo.* Until then, contrast the written forms in isolation.

## Practice items

**P-2.1** — Add the correct definite article: ___ mapa, ___ mano, ___ ciudad, ___ problema, ___ noche.
**Key:** *el mapa, la mano, la ciudad, el problema, la noche.*

**P-2.2** — Make plural: *el papel, la lección, un lápiz, una amiga.*
**Key:** *los papeles, las lecciones, unos lápices, unas amigas.*

**P-2.3** — Correct the errors: \**La problema es la día.*
**Key:** *El problema es el día.* (Both are masculine exceptions.)

**P-2.4** — Choose: *(Un / Una) foto de (el / él) y su amigo.*
**Key:** *Una foto de él y su amigo.* (*foto* is feminine → *Una*; after a preposition use *él* "him," never the article *el*.)

**P-2.5 (production)** — Write three sentences of the form "The ___ is ___" using one regular noun, one -ma word, and one exception word.
**Key (model):** e.g. *La casa es grande. El idioma es difícil. La mano es pequeña.* Check article agreement; adjectives may be any the learner knows (do not require adjective-agreement mastery — that's previewed, not tested, at this point).

## Scope notes

- Adjective agreement is used lightly in examples but is not an objective of this unit; correct gently, don't drill it here.
- Do not introduce *lo* (neuter article) or contractions *al/del*.
