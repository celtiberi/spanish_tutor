# Unit 1 — Greetings, Introductions, Courtesy

## Learning objectives

By the end of this unit the learner can:

1. Greet and take leave appropriately for the time of day and formality level.
2. Introduce themselves and ask someone's name, in both informal (*tú*) and formal (*usted*) registers.
3. Ask and answer "how are you?" with three basic responses.
4. Use core courtesy words (*por favor, gracias, de nada, perdón*).

## Canonical explanation

### Greetings by time of day

| Spanish | Use |
|---------|-----|
| **Hola** | Any time; neutral "hello" |
| **Buenos días** | Morning, until roughly midday |
| **Buenas tardes** | Afternoon, until dark |
| **Buenas noches** | Evening/night — used both to greet and to say good night |

Note the pattern: **buenos** with **días** (masculine), **buenas** with **tardes/noches** (feminine). This previews Unit 2 gender agreement; at this stage learners memorize the fixed phrases.

### Introductions

| Spanish | English | Register |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Me llamo...** | My name is... | both |
| **Soy...** | I am... | both |
| **¿Cómo te llamas?** | What's your name? | informal (*tú*) |
| **¿Cómo se llama (usted)?** | What's your name? | formal (*usted*) |
| **Mucho gusto** | Nice to meet you | both |
| **Encantado / Encantada** | Delighted (male / female speaker) | both |

*Me llamo* literally means "I call myself" — learners do not need the grammar yet, only the fixed forms *me llamo / te llamas / se llama*.

### How are you?

| Spanish | English | Register |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **¿Cómo estás?** | How are you? | informal |
| **¿Cómo está (usted)?** | How are you? | formal |
| **Bien, gracias. ¿Y tú? / ¿Y usted?** | Fine, thanks. And you? | — |
| **Muy bien / Más o menos / Mal** | Very well / So-so / Bad | — |

### Formal vs informal: tú and usted

- **tú** — friends, family, children, peers.
- **usted** — strangers, elders, professional settings; abbreviated *Ud.* Politeness norms vary by country, but A1 learners should default to *usted* with adult strangers.

### Farewells

**Adiós** (goodbye), **Hasta luego** (see you later), **Hasta mañana** (see you tomorrow), **Buenas noches** (good night).

### Courtesy

**Por favor** (please), **Gracias** (thank you), **Muchas gracias** (thank you very much), **De nada** (you're welcome), **Perdón / Disculpe** (sorry / excuse me, formal).

## Examples and non-examples

- ✅ *Buenos días, señora. ¿Cómo está usted?* — morning, formal.
- ✅ *Hola, Marta. ¿Cómo estás?* — informal, any time.
- ❌ *Buenos noches* — wrong agreement; the fixed phrase is *Buenas noches*.
- ❌ *¿Cómo te llamas, señor director?* — register clash: use *¿Cómo se llama usted?* with a formal title.
- ❌ *Encantado* said by a female speaker — she says *Encantada*.

## Common misconceptions

### M-1.1 — "Buenos" and "buenas" are interchangeable
Learners say *buenos tardes* or *buenas días*. **Diagnosis cue:** wrong -os/-as ending on greetings. **Remediation:** these are fixed phrases; *días* takes *buenos*, *tardes* and *noches* take *buenas*. Have the learner produce all three correctly twice.

### M-1.2 — "¿Cómo estás?" works for everyone
Learners use *tú* forms with people they should address formally. **Diagnosis cue:** *estás / te llamas* directed at a boss, elder, or stranger in a roleplay. **Remediation:** contrast the pair (*¿Cómo estás?* ↔ *¿Cómo está usted?*), then re-run the roleplay with a formal interlocutor.

### M-1.3 — "Buenas noches" is only for bedtime
Learners avoid *buenas noches* as an evening greeting, thinking it means only "good night (I'm leaving/sleeping)." **Diagnosis cue:** learner greets at night with *buenas tardes* or hesitates. **Remediation:** *buenas noches* both opens and closes an evening encounter; context does the work.

### M-1.4 — "Mucho gusto" means "much taste" so it must be about food
Rare but real literal-translation trap. **Diagnosis cue:** learner translates *gusto* literally or misuses the phrase outside introductions. **Remediation:** teach it as a fixed formula said when meeting someone new; the reply is *igualmente* or *mucho gusto* back.

## Practice items

**P-1.1** — It is 9 a.m. Greet your teacher (a woman you address formally) and ask how she is.
**Key:** *Buenos días, señora. ¿Cómo está (usted)?* — accept any formal variant with *buenos días*.

**P-1.2** — Fill in: "___ tardes. Me ___ Carlos. ¿Cómo te ___?"
**Key:** *Buenas / llamo / llamas.*

**P-1.3** — Your new classmate says: *Mucho gusto.* Give two possible replies.
**Key:** *Igualmente* or *Mucho gusto* (also accept *Encantado/Encantada*).

**P-1.4** — Translate for the right register: "What is your name?" asked to (a) a child, (b) a customs officer.
**Key:** (a) *¿Cómo te llamas?* (b) *¿Cómo se llama (usted)?*

**P-1.5 (production)** — Write a 4-line dialogue: two strangers meet in the evening, greet, exchange names, and part.
**Key (model answer):** *— Buenas noches. — Buenas noches. ¿Cómo se llama usted? — Me llamo Ana, ¿y usted? — Soy Luis. Mucho gusto. — Igualmente. Hasta luego.* Accept variations with correct register and agreement.

## Scope notes

- Do not introduce *ser* vs *estar* theory here (Units 3–4); *soy* and *estás* appear only inside fixed phrases.
- Do not teach reflexive-verb grammar behind *me llamo*; it is a formula at this stage.
