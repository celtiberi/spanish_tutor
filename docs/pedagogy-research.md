## 2. Area-by-area: modern evidence → pack/policy bets

### A. CLT and TBLT

**Modern practice/evidence:** Communicative Language Teaching (CLT) prioritizes meaningful interaction; Task-Based Language Teaching (TBLT) organizes learning around **tasks** (information gap, problem solve, roleplay with outcome) with **focus-on-form** during or after meaning work, not pre-planned structure syllabi (Long’s FonF vs FonFs). Grammar appears as needed for task success, not as the unit of progression.

**Pack bets:** Units are grammar topics (*ser*, *estar*, regular present…); practice is mostly fill-in / conjugate / correct-the-error / short controlled dialogue (P-1.5, P-4.5). One production dialogue is not a task (no information gap, no real-world outcome beyond “write 4–6 turns”).

| Bet | Ruling |
|-----|--------|
| Grammar-core sequencing | **REFUTE as modern CLT/TBLT alignment** — classic PPP/FonFs. Acceptable as **explicit FFI slice**, not as “communicative A1 course.” |
| Drill-with-keys practice | **PARTIALLY VERIFY** for controlled accuracy; **REFUTE** as sufficient communicative practice. |
| Socratic hints before answers | **VERIFY** as compatible with prompt-style CF / guided discovery; not TBLT-specific. |

### B. Comprehensible input (Krashen) and TPRS

**Modern standing:** Comprehensible input remains **necessary** for acquisition; pure Input Hypothesis claims (i+1 only, monitor useless, affective filter as primary) are **overclaimed** relative to instructed-SLA metas. TPRS/CI products (e.g. Dreaming Spanish as of 2026) deliver high-volume graded listening and explicitly **de-emphasize grammar**. Meta-analytic evidence favors **blends**: input + form focus beats pure implicit for measurable instructed gains (Norris & Ortega 2000; Goo et al. 2015; Kang et al. 2019).

**Pack bets:** Almost no extended Spanish input; explanations in English; Spanish appears as exemplars and learner production targets. No story-based input cycle, no listening bank, no “i+1 narrative.”

| Bet | Ruling |
|-----|--------|
| Explicit-first, low-input design | **REFUTE** pure-CI products; **VERIFY** against ISLA metas that value explicit FFI — but pack **under-delivers input** even by hybrid standards. |
| Closed vocabulary sets | **PARTIALLY VERIFY** for early control and diagnostic validity; **REFUTE** as sufficient for CI or CEFR range (A1 often estimated ~hundreds to ~1k+ items depending on source; Nation-type mappings treat A1 as a small survival repertoire, but products still flood beginners with situational lexis). |

### C. Explicit vs implicit; FonF vs FonFs; Norris & Ortega line

**Evidence:**  
- Norris & Ortega (2000): focused instruction large effect (*d* ≈ 0.96); explicit > implicit; FonF and FonFs both large on then-typical measures.  
- Goo et al. (2015 update): explicit *g* ≈ 1.29, implicit *g* ≈ 0.77 → explicit advantage **~0.52** Hedges’ *g* on average (1.29 − 0.77 = 0.52).  
- Kang et al. (2019): overall *g* = 1.06 (95% CI 0.84–1.29); **minor** explicit–implicit difference when broader outcome modes considered; beginners often benefit strongly from FFI.

**Pack bets:** Explicit decision tables (Unit 4 what/how/where), misconception remediation, English metalanguage, conjugation drills — pure **FonFs + explicit knowledge**.

### D. Retrieval practice and spacing

**Evidence:** Testing effect and spaced practice are robust in cognitive psychology; Kim & Webb (2022) meta-analysis: spacing benefits L2 learning medium–large; longer gaps help delayed retention more than short gaps. Within-session re-test is weak spacing.

**Policy bets:** “Never answer before attempt,” two genuine attempts + escalating hints, `revisit_queue` for same-session re-test, “Recap & space.”

| Bet | Ruling |
|-----|--------|
| Attempt-before-reveal / retrieval | **VERIFY** — aligns with retrieval practice and Lyster-style prompts (push output before giving form). |

| Two-attempt reveal gate | **PARTIALLY VERIFY** — protects from over-help; risk of **frustration / cognitive overload** if hints stay metalinguistic rather than providing better input exemplars. |

## 4. What the (pack/policy) design MISSED

Not “bugs in Spanish,” but **research-shaped holes**:

1. **No task layer.** No information-gap, role outcome, or real communicative need beyond writing model dialogues. Modern TBLT/CEFR would demand at least one **can-do task per unit** with success criteria other than key match.

2. **Starved input.** No graded listening/reading bank; minutes of Spanish input per unit are tiny. Even hybrid programs (Babbel, modern Duolingo) front-load dialogue; CI products make input the engine. Hybrid evidence still requires **substantial** input alongside FFI.

3. **Spacing is theatrical, not scheduled.** `revisit_queue` is same-session. Durable L2 gains want **multi-day** expanding intervals (hours→days→weeks), not only end-of-segment recap.

4. **Outcome measures are almost all controlled production / metalinguistic justification.** Unit 4 even asks “justify in one word (what/how/where)” — excellent for explicit knowledge, poor proxy for unplanned oral ability. ISLA metas show mode of measure modulates effect sizes (Kang et al. 2019).

5. **Processing / form–meaning mapping underused.** VanPatten-style **structured input** (choose meaning from form before producing) is missing; pack jumps to conjugate-and-produce. That is the traditional “explanation + output drill” profile PI research critiques.

6. **Lexis domains that carry A1 can-dos are deferred** (food, family, time, *ir*, *hay*). Result: learner can discuss *ser/estar* theory but cannot do many A1 life tasks. Grammar-core honesty does not fix the **coverage hole**.

7. **No oral/phonological practice loop** beyond notes. A1 products and CEFR include phonological control; text-only CLI tutor is a medium constraint that should be stated as a **skill gap**, not assumed neutral.

8. **Motivation / affect / agency.** Policy handles frustration only as over-help resistance. No voluntary topic choice beyond unit menu; closed sets can raise boredom relative to Duolingo skill variety or iTalki personalization.

9. **Register teaching without sociopragmatic range.** Unit 1 *tú/usted* is good; real A1 also needs service encounters, digital messages, regional softness of *usted* norms — pack acknowledges variation but does not practice it richly.

10. **Transfer and interleaving.** Units are blocked (all *estar* then next topic). Spacing research and desirable difficulties favor **interleaved** ser/estar/tener items across days, not only massed unit completion.

## 6. Ranked: 5 highest-impact changes modern research would demand

Ranked by expected impact on **communicative A1 outcomes** (not on grammar-test scores alone).

### 1. Add CEFR-style **tasks** and rewrite unit goals as can-dos with task success criteria  
**Change:** Each unit ends with 1–2 tasks (e.g. “exchange identity and wellbeing with a stranger in a 90-second roleplay”; “locate three family members on a map using *estar*”). Score task completion, not only key match.  
**Why:** CEFR action-oriented approach; TBLT/FonF evidence that form sticks better when needed for meaning.  
**Citations:** Council of Europe CEFR / Companion Volume (2020); Long FonF (1991/1998); Ellis (2016).

### 2. Inject a **comprehensible input block** before or beside drills (mini-dialogues, graded stories, tutor-spoken Spanish minutes)  
**Change:** For every rule table, provide 8–15 turns of recycled Spanish narrative/dialogue at i+1; require comprehension checks **before** conjugation drills.  
**Why:** Input is non-negotiable for acquisition; pure explicit path under-delivers relative to hybrid products; CI products show demand for listening hours.  
**Citations:** Krashen (as necessary-not-sufficient); Spada / FFI reviews; product practice (Babbel dialogue-first; Dreaming Spanish pure CI as contrast).

### 3. Replace pure mechanical drills with **structured input + prompted output** (keep keys, change activity type)  
**Change:** P-4.1-style “conjugate all persons” → prefer: hear/read forms and select location vs identity meaning; then prompted production; save full paradigms for summary.  
**Why:** VanPatten PI line: form–meaning mapping beats explanation + mechanical output for building processable intake; still keeps explicit FFI.  
**Citations:** VanPatten & Cadierno (1993) and later PI research syntheses; Kang et al. (2019) on measure-sensitive FFI gains.

### 4. Implement **true multi-day spacing / interleaving** in pack + policy state  
**Change:** Extend `session_state` with cross-session due items (e.g. failed M-4.1 items reappear after 1 day, 3 days, 7 days); interleave Unit 3–4–6 items rather than blocked mastery gates only.  
**Why:** Kim & Webb (2022): spacing medium–large for L2; longer spacing wins on delayed tests; same-session recap is insufficient.  
**Citations:** Kim & Webb (2022); Cepeda et al. (2006, 2008) spacing literature.

### 5. Soften pure FonFs spine: **situational modules** that pull grammar as needed (keep misconception IDs)  
**Change:** Re-present Units 1–6 as scenarios (meet someone; describe family; say where people are; ask age/time-of-day plans) that **summon** ser/estar/tener, instead of only “Unit 4 = estar chapter.” Keep M-IDs as diagnostic backend.  
**Why:** Aligns with Duolingo functional skills mix, Babbel real-life lessons, and CEFR can-dos while preserving the pack’s best asset (misconception taxonomy).  
**Citations:** CEFR can-do orientation; Duolingo CEFR rebuild notes (2019+); Babbel method (dialogue → notice → explicit tip).

**Honorable near-miss (6th):** Enrich corrective moves so **prompts > recasts** by default (policy already leans Socratic — enforce “learner re-produces full form” after every remediation). Lyster & Saito (2010).
