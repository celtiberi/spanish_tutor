### Q1 — §2.8 law text: **AMEND** (not COUNTERSIGN as drafted)

**Why not pure COUNTERSIGN**

1. Contingent shift is **per failure**, not after a two-fail wait. The draft correctly says “NEXT turn gives more support” after one fail, then smuggles a second, different rule (“two consecutive”) without separating layers. That invites the model to wait for two fails before escalating.
2. “Two consecutive failures” is a **project policy heuristic**, not a literature constant. Promoting it as if research-derived is the same class of error as the Lyster/Ranta citation inversion (2026-07-28).
3. “Grade the struggle… sheet must show…” is honesty-adjacent. Teaching court may say what evidence *means*; it must not invent sheet fields or contradict ENGINEERING §3.2. Wording must stay evidence-honest without sounding like a sheet schema mandate.
4. Introduce freeze while struggling is sound (incident T3; P1/P2) and should stay.

**Exact replacement — §2.8 (teaching-court text to promote)**

```markdown
### 2.8 Support rises when the learner struggles — and fades when they succeed
Contingency is the rule: after a failed, garbled, or clearly stuck attempt on an item, the *next* turn raises support — shorter and simpler Spanish, more English scaffolding when needed, re-model the *same* item, and a smaller ask (recognition or yes/no before free production). After a clear success, hand control back: less support, a bigger ask.
If the learner fails the same item twice in a row even after raised support, stop eliciting production of it for now. Model it again, check comprehension only, and return to something the learner *can* do — bank a success before retrying the hard thing. Novices need mostly-successful practice; a failure streak teaches anxiety, not Spanish.
While the learner is struggling on the current work, introduce nothing new: new items wait until the learner is succeeding again. New items ride on a base of success, never on top of confusion.
Grade honestly: a garbled or uninterpretable attempt is evidence of difficulty or non-evidence for ability — never grounds to mark an ability as emerging. Struggle must not be rewritten as progress.
```

**P10 — Support must be contingent on learner success.** Scaffolding is temporary, adaptive control of task elements the learner cannot yet manage (Wood, Bruner & Ross 1976). ### Q2 — Is “two consecutive failures” the right trigger?

**AMEND framing: keep the number as a production-freeze policy; do not claim literature origin.**

**Arithmetic (Rosenshine ~80%):**

- Target success rate ≈ **0.80** during guided practice.
- Local window of last **2** production attempts: 2 consecutive fails → success rate = \(0/2 = 0.00\) → **0.00 − 0.80 = −0.80** (80 percentage points under target).
- Local window of last **5** with 2 consecutive fails and 3 prior successes: \(3/5 = 0.60\) → **0.60 − 0.80 = −0.20** (still under target).
- After **1** fail alone in a 5-attempt window with 4 successes: \(4/5 = 0.80\) — still at target *if* support already rose on that fail; without escalation, the next fail is free to stack.

**Literature says:** escalate support after **each** failure (contingent shift, turn-level). It does **not** say “freeze production at N=2.”  

**Engineering judgment for this product:**  
- **Layer A (mandatory, research-aligned):** fail once → more support next turn (already in amended text).  
- **Layer B (project policy, acceptable):** after 2 consecutive fails *despite* Layer A, stop production elicitation of that item, check comprehension, bank a success elsewhere.  

N=2 is defensible as a short conversational window (one-to-one tutor, A1), not as a scientific constant. **Do not** change N without a pre-registered live-session bound later. Reject any wording that implies “two fails is when contingency starts.”
